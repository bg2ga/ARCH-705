Robot36 (Ahmet Inan, 0BSD - LICENSE) in HF Digital Modes, for SSTV receive.

Source: https://github.com/xdsopl/robot36 at commit 2af2390 (2026-10-03).

The decoder's classes (Decoder, Demodulator, the modes and the filters they use) are copied unchanged; Robot36's
activity, audio sources, file readers and settings are not used. SstvDecoder.java is the app's own (GPL v3, as the
app): it sits in this package because Decoder's constructor and current mode are package-private, and holds the two
pixel buffers as Robot36's MainActivity does.

SstvDecoder (ours, not Robot36's) now sets the decoder's starting mode to a RawDecoder named "Raw": Robot36 starts in
Robot 36, so the app could not tell "a Robot 36 picture found from its line timing" from "nothing found yet".
Robot36's own files are still unchanged.

VisFinder.java (ours, GPL v3) is a second VIS (start code) search for weak HF signals, and Decoder.java is changed to
use it - the only change to Robot36's own files:
- Off air the 10 ms break and the start bit of the header were nearly always lost: Robot36 reads the header through
  its FM demodulator (the strongest thing in 1000-2800 Hz), which reads garbage once noise is within a few dB of the
  tones, worst at the low header tones. So pictures only started from the line timing, with the top lost.
- VisFinder tracks the strongest single tone (a 30 ms FFT every 1 ms) on the raw audio and looks for a steady leader,
  8 code bits and the stop bit, relative to the leader (mistuning up to 200 Hz); one unreadable bit is given back by
  the parity. It needs neither the break nor the start bit.
- Decoder: process() gives VisFinder the raw audio before the demodulator overwrites it; handleHeader's second half is
  split out as startPicture() (unchanged, but no red lines for an unknown code from VisFinder); handleHeaderFallback()
  starts VisFinder's header when Robot36's own search did not; a started picture holds VisFinder off for a second.
Checked on 10 off-air recordings (2026-10-11): 4 headers found (Robot 36 x1, Scottie 1 x3) where Robot36 found none.

0.18.2: Decoder clears the picture to black when one starts (startPicture), and the scan-line scope when its line
timing finds a new picture (processSyncPulse, pictureChanged): Robot36 left the last picture there, so the app showed
the new one slowly overwriting the old.

0.20.1: a picture found from its line timing (processSyncPulse, pictureChanged to a mode other than Raw, no picture in
progress) is started like one with a VIS code - image buffer sized, line 0, cleared - so it is drawn top-down and
finishes like one (the app then keeps it if most of its lines came with their own sync pulses). Counters for that:
startedByTiming, syncedLines (lines placed by a sync pulse), picLines (lines copied into the picture).

0.20.2: slant. A picture in progress (processSyncPulse) and mode detection by line timing (detectMode) allow the measured
line length to be within 1 % of the mode's (slantTolerance), not just Robot36's fixed 1 ms. A sound card a little fast
or slow makes every line longer or shorter; past 1 ms (0.36 % in Scottie 2, 0.1 % in the longest modes) every sync was
refused and the picture carried on at the nominal length, slanting. Checked by resampling an off-air Scottie 2: at
0.5 % it slanted badly (1 % of its syncs used); now straight to 0.8 % either way (77-81 % used).

0.20.3: line tracking from every sync pulse (trackSync). On the user's USB recordings the slant was not a refused
line length: the lines were right (~0.23 % long), but most sync pulses were lost or measured at the wrong width (a 9 ms
Scottie sync filed as 5 / 20 ms), so Robot36's "four lines in a row agree" never held and the picture ran on at the
nominal length. Now, inside a picture, every detected pulse (any width) within 5 % of a line of the grid (then 4 ms of
the fit) joins a least-squares fit of pulse position against line number; its slope sets the line length and it
places the next line's start. The fit's positions move with shiftSamples. EA4ADJ's Scottie 2 and EC7C's Martin 2 (off
air, USB) came out straight and whole instead of torn diagonals; the other recordings decode as before.
